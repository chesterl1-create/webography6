      <td><strong>Abstract</strong></td>

      <td>This report from the Brennan Center for Justice explains how predictive policing tools use algorithms and historical records to forecast crime locations or individuals associated with elevated risk. It distinguishes between place-based forecasting that maps prior incident patterns and person-based models that score arrest or victimization risk factors. While noting arguments about resource efficiency and moving beyond subjective hunches, the analysis explains how training data shaped by past policing practices can perpetuate racial disparities. It also points out that lack of transparency and independent evaluations have led several departments to end or limit predictive policing initiatives. This resource offers clear analysis of the civil rights risks and empirical limitations of algorithmic forecasting in policing.</td>

      <td><strong>Abstract</strong></td>

      <td>This educational article from American Military University introduces how data analytics, social network analysis, and cloud computing support predictive policing efforts across law enforcement agencies. It outlines potential operational uses such as patrol scheduling, violence reduction, investigative prioritization, and threat monitoring aimed at helping agencies allocate limited resources proactively. At the same time, the article raises concerns about surveillance creep, public accountability, and constitutional probable cause standards. It stresses that algorithmic outputs should serve as a decision-support aid rather than replacing human judgment, legal standards, and community oversight. Readers can use this overview to examine practical applications alongside legal and ethical debates surrounding predictive algorithms in criminal justice.</td>
